    How GST is calculated

    Adding GST: GST = amount × rate ÷ 100, and total = amount + GST. For example, 18% GST on ₹1,000 is ₹180, so the total is ₹1,180.

    Removing GST (reverse GST): net = inclusive amount × 100 ÷ (100 + rate), and GST = inclusive amount − net. ₹1,180 including 18% GST is ₹1,000 plus ₹180 GST.

    CGST, SGST and IGST: a sale within one state is split equally into central GST (CGST) and state GST (SGST, or UTGST in some union territories), so 18% becomes 9% + 9%. A sale to another state carries the full rate as integrated GST (IGST).

    GST rates: from 22 September 2025 most goods and services fall into two slabs, 5% and 18%, with 40% for luxury and sin goods. Gold and silver are 3%, rough diamonds 0.25%, and many essentials are exempt. The old 12% and 28% slabs are kept here for older bills. Check the exact rate for your HSN or SAC code on the CBIC website.

    Frequently asked questions

    How much is 18% GST on ₹1,000?

    ₹180, so the total is ₹1,180. Within a state that is ₹90 CGST and ₹90 SGST; to another state it is ₹180 IGST.

    How do I remove GST from a total amount?

    Divide by (1 + rate ÷ 100). For 18%, divide by 1.18: ₹1,180 ÷ 1.18 = ₹1,000 before GST. Choose "Including GST" above to do it automatically.

    What are the new GST rates?

    Since 22 September 2025 the main GST slabs are 5% and 18%, with a 40% rate for luxury and sin goods, 3% for gold and silver and 0.25% for rough diamonds. The 12% and 28% slabs were merged into these.

    When do I charge IGST instead of CGST and SGST?

    Charge IGST when the place of supply is in a different state from the supplier, and CGST + SGST when both are in the same state.

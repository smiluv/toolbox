    How India's income tax is calculated

    New regime (default): slabs of 0% up to ₹4 lakh, 5% to ₹8 lakh, 10% to ₹12 lakh, 15% to ₹16 lakh, 20% to ₹20 lakh, 25% to ₹24 lakh and 30% above. Salaried people get a ₹75,000 standard deduction, and the section 87A rebate of up to ₹60,000 makes taxable income up to ₹12 lakh tax-free (₹12.75 lakh salary), with marginal relief just above that. Most deductions such as 80C, 80D and HRA are not allowed.

    Old regime: 0% up to ₹2.5 lakh (₹3 lakh for seniors, ₹5 lakh for 80+), 5% to ₹5 lakh, 20% to ₹10 lakh and 30% above, with a ₹50,000 standard deduction, a ₹12,500 rebate up to ₹5 lakh taxable income, and deductions like 80C, 80D, HRA, home loan interest and NPS.

    Both regimes add a surcharge on high incomes (10% above ₹50 lakh, 15% above ₹1 crore, 25% above ₹2 crore, and 37% above ₹5 crore in the old regime only) with marginal relief, plus 4% health and education cess. This is an estimate: check your figures on the Income Tax Department website or with a chartered accountant. Other presets use simple progressive brackets without credits or social security.

    Frequently asked questions

    Which tax regime is better for me, new or old?

    The new regime has lower rates but almost no deductions; the old regime has higher rates but lets you claim 80C, 80D, HRA, home loan interest and more. The calculator compares both and shows the deductions you would need for the old regime to break even.

    Is income up to ₹12 lakh tax-free in the new regime?

    Yes. Taxable income up to ₹12 lakh gets a full 87A rebate, so salaried people pay no tax up to ₹12.75 lakh gross after the ₹75,000 standard deduction. Just above the limit, marginal relief caps the tax at the income above ₹12 lakh.

    Can I switch between the new and old regime?

    Salaried people without business income can choose either regime every year when filing their return. People with business income can switch back from the new regime only once.

    What is the difference between effective and marginal tax rate?

    The marginal rate is the rate on your last rupee or dollar of income. The effective rate is total tax divided by total income, which is always lower in a progressive system.

    How the home loan EMI is calculated

    The loan amount is the property price minus your down payment. With compound (reducing balance) interest the EMI is P·r·(1 + r)n / ((1 + r)n − 1), where P is the loan, n the number of months and r the monthly rate (for non-monthly compounding, r = (1 + R/k)k/12 − 1). With fixed (flat rate) interest the total interest is P × R × years, and the EMI is (P + total interest) / n. The APR solves for the reducing-balance rate that gives the same EMI on the money you actually receive (loan minus fees), so you can compare offers fairly.

    Any additional monthly payment goes straight to principal while the EMI stays the same, so the loan ends earlier. The rest period decides when the bank revises interest on the new balance: with a monthly rest each month's interest is worked out on last month's balance, with a yearly rest it stays based on the balance at the start of the year, so prepayments only start saving interest the following year.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is an EMI?

    EMI stands for equated monthly installment: the fixed amount you pay every month, covering both interest and principal, until the loan is repaid.

    What is the difference between flat rate and reducing balance interest?

    A flat (fixed) rate charges interest on the full original loan for the entire term, even though you repay part of it every month. A reducing balance (compound) rate charges interest only on what you still owe, so each month's interest gets smaller. At the same headline rate, a flat rate costs much more.

    Is a flat rate or a reducing rate better?

    For the borrower, a reducing balance rate is cheaper at the same number. A flat rate is roughly equivalent to a reducing rate 1.8 times higher on short loans, and about 1.3 to 1.6 times higher on long home loans. Compare offers using the APR shown above, not the headline rate.

    What does compounding frequency change?

    Most home loans compound monthly. Daily reducing loans charge interest on the daily balance, which works out slightly higher than monthly at the same nominal rate; quarterly or yearly compounding works out slightly lower.

    How much do extra monthly payments save?

    On a reducing balance loan every extra amount lowers the principal, so all future interest is charged on a smaller balance and the loan finishes years earlier. For example, 200,000 at 6% for 30 years paid off with an extra 200 a month finishes in about 21 years and saves almost 80,000 in interest. Ask your bank whether it charges a partial prepayment fee.

    What is monthly rest vs yearly rest?

    The rest period is how often the bank recalculates interest on your outstanding balance. With a monthly rest (standard today) a prepayment lowers interest from the next month. With a yearly rest (annual reducing) interest stays based on the balance at the start of the year, so you pay slightly more interest overall and prepayments help only from the next year.

    Is a shorter or longer home loan better?

    A shorter tenure means higher monthly payments but far less total interest. A longer tenure lowers the EMI but you pay much more interest overall. Use the tenure buttons or slider and watch the chart change.

    How much down payment do I need?

    It depends on the country and lender. Many banks ask for 10–25% of the price; in the UAE expatriate first-time buyers typically need at least 20%, and in the US 20% avoids private mortgage insurance.
